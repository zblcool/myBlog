# Editorial Roadmap

## Core Positioning

Ash Zhang's site should be treated as a personal brand site with two equally important lanes:

1. Durable technical writing and learning notes.
2. Interactive graphics, game, and product-flavored portfolio work.

That means the site should not read like a generic blog and should not read like a pure portfolio either. The strongest framing is:

`personal brand site + technical writing archive + interactive graphics showcase`

## Audience Priority

1. Creative technologists, collaborators, and hiring managers who want to understand Ash's taste and technical direction quickly.
2. Readers who arrive through posts and want practical explanations, especially around graphics, front-end systems, and JavaScript.
3. Future collaborators who need a compact overview of projects, writing voice, and experimentation range.

## Content Roles

- `Posts` should hold polished, durable writing with a clear takeaway.
- `Notes` should remain useful even when rough, but they should not compete with posts for the same editorial job.
- `Portfolio` should lead with interactive or systems-heavy work first, then concept and archive pieces.
- `CV` should stay concise and role-oriented.
- `Tools` should show small product experiments without pretending to be essays.

## Notes Triage

These recommendations are meant to keep the archive useful without forcing every note into a polished article.

### Keep As Raw Study Notes

- `VueX Notes`: useful historical study material, but not central to the current stack.
- `Mathematics in CS`: keep as reference notes unless a stronger graphics-focused angle emerges.
- `Machine learning`: keep as archive notes for breadth, not as a near-term article priority.
- `Database 101`: useful as a study note, but too broad to polish without a sharper hook.
- `Foudamental of Software Devleopment`: keep as a study archive unless split into narrower lessons.
- `Divide and Conquer`: keep as a course-style note.
- `C++ Notes`: keep as rough notes unless tied to graphics or engine work.
- `Tableau`: keep as archive material.
- `Software testing`: keep as notes unless rewritten around practical front-end testing decisions.

### Promote Into Polished Posts

- `Computer Graphics Notes`:
  Turn into a clearer article or article series around graphics intuition, visual systems, or rendering ideas that show up in web work.
- `React Notes`:
  Refresh into a post about what still matters from React fundamentals in a content-first, Astro-first site.
- `Algorithms Notes`:
  Promote only if rewritten around practical patterns, not as generic lecture notes.
- `Do not use forEach with async-await. Use For loop instead`:
  This already has a strong article-shaped hook and should become a polished JavaScript post soon.
